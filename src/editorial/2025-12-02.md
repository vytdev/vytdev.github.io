# Toxic Metals Found in Alarming Levels in Kid's Toys

Researchers from Brazil have found significant amounts of hazardous metals in
popular children's toys. The team examined 70 products, including imported
and locally produced items. Based on their assessment, the extraction rates
&mdash; referring to how much of these metals leaches out under conditions
mimicking oral contact &mdash; ranged from 0.11% to 7.33%. Despite that these
amounts are tiny, the researchers noted that these don't eliminate health
concerns.

Their study revealed 21 toxic elements from the toys, notably lead (Pb),
chromium (Cr), and antimony (Sb), which are known to contribute to various
health problems. Those include irreversible neurological harm, memory problems,
gastrointestinal problems, and more. The team recognized similarities in their
results, suggesting a common manufacturing origin. "Beige-colored toys had
higher metal concentrations, possibly due to the paint supplier, which is a
relevant clue for future enforcement actions", says Bruno Alves Rocha.

The researchers said it is not the first study with these alarming results,
and that this only reinforces the need for urgent actions to protect children's
health. These findings show oversight in manufacturing, and how enforcing
stricter rules in such matters is important, even for the things we wouldn't
expect to be significant.
